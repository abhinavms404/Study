Botnets

A botnet is a network of compromised computers (bots/zombies) controlled by a single attacker, used for DDoS attacks, spam, and data theft.

DDoS Attacks

Botnets flood target servers with massive traffic, overwhelming them and causing service outages. Mirai botnet reached 1.2 Tbps attack volume.

📧

Spam & Phishing

Mass distribution of spam emails and phishing campaigns through thousands of compromised machines simultaneously.

💰

Cryptomining

Secretly using victims' computing resources to mine cryptocurrency, causing performance degradation and increased power costs.

🔑

Credential Stuffing

Using botnets to test stolen username/password pairs across thousands of sites, exploiting password reuse.

💳

Click Fraud

Automatically clicking online ads to generate fraudulent revenue for attackers, costing advertisers billions.

🔓

Brute Force Attacks

Distributing login attempts across botnet nodes to crack passwords without triggering rate limits.

🤖 Notable Botnets

  • Mirai (2016) — 600K+ IoT bots, attacked Dyn DNS
  • Emotet (2014) — Banking trojan turned botnet-as-a-service
  • TrickBot (2016) — Modular malware, 1M+ infected
  • BazarLoader (2020) — Ryuk ransomware delivery
  • Mozi (2019) — P2P botnet, DDoS & data theft

🛡️ Defense Against Botnets

  • Change default passwords on IoT devices
  • Network segmentation & monitoring
  • DNS filtering to block C&C traffic
  • Regular firmware updates
  • Intrusion Detection Systems (IDS)

Types of Threat Actors

Understanding the motivations, capabilities, and tactics of different threat actor groups is critical for effective defense.

🏴

Nation-State Actors

Political / Espionage
  • Well-funded, patient, sophisticated
  • Zero-day exploits, custom malware
  • Targets: government, defense, critical infra
  • Examples: APT28 (Russia), APT41 (China), Lazarus (DPRK)
  • Dwell time: 200+ days average
💰

Cybercriminals

Financial Gain
  • Organized crime groups, RaaS operators
  • Ransomware, banking trojans, fraud
  • Target: any organization with money
  • Examples: REvil, Conti, FIN7, Carbanak
  • Increasingly targeting healthcare & education
✊

Hacktivists

Ideology / Political
  • Motivated by social/political causes
  • Defacement, DDoS, data leaks
  • Target: governments, corporations
  • Examples: Anonymous, LulzSec, KillNet
  • Often use social media for coordination
🕵️

Insider Threats

Revenge / Financial
  • Current/former employees with access
  • Malicious or negligent insiders
  • Target: their own organization
  • Difficult to detect, bypass perimeter security
  • 40% of breaches involve insiders
🧒

Script Kiddies

Thrill / Notoriety
  • Low technical skill, use pre-made tools
  • Download exploit kits, follow tutorials
  • Target: easy, vulnerable systems
  • Impact: usually low but annoying
  • May escalate skill over time
🎯

Advanced Persistent Threats

Long-term Espionage
  • Prolonged, targeted campaigns
  • Multi-stage attacks, custom tooling
  • Target: high-value intellectual property
  • Examples: APT29 (Cozy Bear), Equation Group
  • Months to years of persistent access
